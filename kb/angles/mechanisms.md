Definition: "the specific reason your product works when everything else the customer has tried did not." Gives a reason to believe this time is different. Main tool at sophistication 3–4.

It must answer: How does it work? Why did past attempts fail? Why is this different?

# 4 types [d:what-are-mechanisms]
1. Ingredient: source, purity/concentration, form (raw, activated, fermented), combination, processing. Most supplement mechanisms live here; often no reformulation is needed.
2. Process: step sequence, activation timing, patented method, preparation.
3. Delivery: absorption, new application, proprietary delivery, targeting. Delivery + ingredient is the most common stack.
4. Product type: new category (mushroom coffee vs coffee), lower-friction format (patch vs pill), removing a harmful element.
- Stack two types when possible. The mechanism often already exists in the product.
- Examples:
  - Seed DS-01: ViaCap capsule-in-capsule. "Probiotics don't work for me" → "they were dead before reaching my gut."
  - MUD\WTR: caffeine is the problem; coffee was "borrowing tomorrow's energy."

# Quality checklist (all 4 must pass)
1. Explains the past failure.
2. Simple: explainable in 1–2 sentences, understood in 10–15 seconds.
3. Truth factor: does it make sense? Is it provable?
4. Removal test: does the messaging collapse without it?
- #1 mistake: overcomplicating it. The best mechanisms "sound like common sense."
- "Your hook earns the stop. Your mechanism earns the belief." Shift blame away from the customer.

# Workflow (course Mechanism Prompt) [d:mechanism-prompt]
Inputs: product name, category, URL, product blueprint. Role: "You are not writing ad copy. You are building the factual foundation."

1. Customer + failed solutions (table):
   - Customer: who has already spent money, been let down, and what they now believe.
   - Numbered failed solutions, each with a specific practical reason it failed that can be proven at a minimum (e.g. retinol conversion rate; collagen molecules too large for the skin barrier).
   - Weakness type: diluted, outdated process, missing co-factor, and so on.
   - The current fix: how this product type bypasses the bottleneck.
2. Differentiators (table):
   - Ingredient, process, tech, ratio, extraction, format, or sourcing.
   - Edge type, stated concretely: faster, more potent, better absorption, fewer side effects.
3. Fresh mechanism angles:
   - Brainstorm from delivery, origins, formulation, protocol, format, and contrarian takes.
   - Discard anything competitors already use heavily. Keep only "I haven't heard that before."
4. Final mechanism:
   - Failed-solution explanation (1–2 sentences, blames the product, not the customer).
   - Statement template: "While most [category] products [specific flaw], [Product] uses [differentiator] to [advantage]. That's why it produces [specific result] instead of [what generics deliver]."
   - 2–3 problem-aware hook directions per mechanism: visible problem → blame shift to the hidden cause → mechanism as the logical fix. Never name the product. Example: "Your under-eye circles are not from lack of sleep. They're from a protein your body stops producing properly after 30."
- Final test: would "a burned, ad-fatigued customer" think "that explains why the other stuff failed and why this might actually work"?
- Vocabulary (research doc only): penetration, bridge, delivery system, binding agent, concentration, pathway, absorption rate, carrier. Banned: revolutionary, synergy, unlock, matrix, game-changer. Translate to plain words in customer copy (house-rules.md).
- Output → products/<p>/mechanism.yaml.

# Placement and risk
- Mechanisms live mainly in ads (esp. video) and landing pages; keep them light on the PDP [c:08-24]. Explain them simply to raise perceived value [c:09-14].
- In a teardown, find the exact mechanism sentence [c:05-14].
- ⚠ Gimmick or unprovable mechanisms carry liability (patch absorption claims) [c:09-24]. New delivery formats (powders, jellies; liquids are regulated) are a legitimate differentiator [c:10-08].
