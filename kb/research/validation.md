# Demand signals (stack several)
- TAM: 3–5M people minimum for a niche product [c:07-23]; "in the millions" [c:08-06]; 10M+ audience preferred for delivery [c:09-21]. Example: a 7M US TAM got exhausted [c:06-18].
- Active Reddit, YouTube, forum discussion. Activity in the last 3–5 months matters more than old content [c:10-22].
- Google Trends rising or stable (short dip OK, sustained decline not) [c:06-29].
- Competitor ad activity, and how varied their angles are [c:08-06].
- AI estimates of market size are a supplement only [c:07-23].

# Believability
- The product must plausibly solve the avatar's core problem. Example: an immunity supplement doesn't fix a dirty warehouse, so reframe the cause or change avatar [c:08-06].
- Multi-ingredient supplements: verify on forums/YouTube/Reddit that the ingredient is actually used for this problem [c:06-25].

# Saturation
- List competitor angles; find unused ones on YouTube; declining competitor traction is a warning [c:08-06].
- Don't copy big players' angles: you lose the bidding war [c:07-09, c:07-23].

# Sub-avatar scorecard (pass all 4) [d:avatar-build-scorecard]
| Criterion | Pass |
|---|---|
| Desire strength | HIGH |
| Competition | LOW–MED |
| Market size ("TAM for $1M/mo") | YES |
| Product works well for this problem/sub-avatar | YES |

# Ad problem or product problem?
- Before scrapping: check campaign setup, purchase optimization, geo, ad quality [c:07-23].
- Pivot thresholds (stage-dependent; no single number):
  - Per avatar: 3–5 ads per angle × 4–5 angles with no traction → switch avatar/problem [c:08-06].
  - Per product: 10–15 batches over 2–3 weeks → consider pivoting [c:09-14].
  - ⚠ Counterpoint: Jordy needed ~50 batches for his first winner [c:08-27]. Pivot only if creative quality was genuinely good (scripts passed script-standards.md).
- Typical funnel of wins: 5 avatars tested → 1 converts → 4 angles → 2–3 sell → mine the winner [c:10-15].
