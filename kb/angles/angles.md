Required inputs: sub-avatar profile, product blueprint, desires (desires.yaml). Mechanism (mechanism.yaml) is strongly recommended. The course prompt omits it, but it feeds "unique root cause".

# Working formula [n:ball]
Angle = core desire (emotional undertone) + problem being solved + format.

Example (ADHD parents):
- "Here's why your child can't focus on their homework": Problem Aware, VO + B-roll, confusion/fear.
- "I tried home schooling, tutoring, and rewards and STILL…": Problem Aware, UGC testimonial, frustration/anger.

Tests:
- Would I BELIEVE this claim if I were the avatar?
- Write as the avatar venting to a therapist or friend.
- The avatar must feel heard AND see logically how the product gets them there.

Terminology: a new customer group is a new AVATAR; angles live inside an avatar [n:ball].

# What an angle is
- "NOT just a hook or a benefit. It's the complete story idea." Like "5 different people in a support group, each telling a COMPLETELY DIFFERENT story."
- Each needs:
  - a trigger moment,
  - a specific transformation,
  - the sub-avatar's exact language,
  - a target awareness level.
- One product can carry 10+ angles. A winning ad commits to one angle, stateable in one sentence [c:05-14].

# Step 1: generate 3–5 angles
For each, mine:
- Primary desire (from desires.yaml).
- Trigger moment: from trigger moments, "day in the life – before" peaks, fear scenario.
- Unique emotional entry point: a relationship, a location/event, a realization, or a betrayal.

Output format:
```
ANGLE #N: [Name]
Target desire:
Story structure: Beginning → Trigger moment → Solution discovery/explanation → Resolution/CTA
Unique root cause:
Unique solution:
Emotionally triggering moment:
Awareness level:
```
Example: "The Baby Shower Countdown". LED mask; pregnancy skin; baby-shower invite 4 weeks away; photographer mentions LED masks.

Differentiation check (all must pass):
- different trigger moment,
- different hidden enemy/mechanism,
- different life circumstance (could be a different person),
- different desire aspect or a different top-5 desire,
- no two angles are variations of each other,
- each is built from real quotes.

Then the user picks one, and the AI asks whether there is reference story copy (hook or body) to model.

# Mapping one avatar across awareness levels [n:ball img10]
Example: parents of kids with sensory issues + a vibration plate.
- Unaware: "If your child covers their ears, this might be why."
- Problem Aware: "You've tried quiet time, deep breathing, and your patience has worn thin. The problem isn't you or their emotions. It's their sensory system" → pitch.
- Solution Aware: eliminate alternatives ("3 fidgeting solutions that don't actually work").
- Product Aware: benefits, testimonials, social proof.

# Native storytelling image ads: key components [n:ball img32]
- Looks like a real user's post.
- The image is eye-catching (gross, interesting, funny, scary) but STILL natural. It shows the problem, the desired outcome, or someone who looks like the sub-avatar ("that's exactly what my problem looks like").
- The copy matches the image and calls out the problem or solution.
- The click goes to a congruent page that EDUCATES before selling. Don't send a skin-problem image straight to a buy page.

# Step 2 (native image ads only): 1,100–1,400-word FB primary-text story
For a "normal/organic looking image… a post from a real user".

Style:
- 2–3-sentence paragraphs, talking "to a friend in front of you".
- No hyphens or AI-like punctuation, no scientific/marketing terms, no section labels in the output.

Structure:
1. Hook (< 25 words, visceral, calls out the problem so the image viewer knows it's for them). Draws on trigger moments and daily struggles.
2. Failed path. Draws on failures and dislikes. Tone: "You're not crazy for trying that – you were lied to."
3. Unique mechanism of the problem: an "aha" that reframes it and shows why the obvious fixes fail.
4. Solution entry point (doctor/expert revelation, personal aha, friend's transformation, accident). NOT a pitch.
5. Soft resolution + CTA to learn more.

# Angle rules from calls
- Don't copy big players' angles; you'll lose the bid [c:07-09].
- Don't build angles from a handful of comments. Use comments for the PDP and offer [c:10-05].
- Name the belief shift each angle must create (e.g. creatine + hair-loss fear → an ingredient that prevents it) [c:07-06].
- One pain point per script [c:09-17].
- Statics test more angles per dollar, with the same rigor as video [c:08-06, c:08-17].
- After a winner: mine that angle (iterations, new hooks, new formats) before going wide [c:10-15].
